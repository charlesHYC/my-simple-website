A High-Efficiency 100 Gbps Traffic Generator and Flow Analysis SoC

Presented at ISEECS 2026, the 9th International Symposium on Electrical Engineering and Computer Science, 5–6 March 2026, Chung Yuan Christian University. Advised by Prof. Yu-Kuen Lai, Department of Electrical Engineering.

TSMC 90 nm Cell-based flow Verilog AXI4-Stream LFSR Count-sketch Gate-level simulation

The problem

100 Gigabit Ethernet is now an ordinary interface, so traffic monitoring and performance measurement increasingly move into the hardware itself. An SoC that carries both gets full observability without an external measurement rig attached to it.

Putting traffic generation and sketch-based analysis on one chip is the hard part. Sustaining 100 Gbps needs a wide datapath and a fast clock, which drives up timing and control complexity, and the analysis counters have to stay correct while several updates contend for the same memory.

How it works

One chip holds a programmable traffic generator and the flow feature analysis for the device under test, with every module talking over AXI4-Stream.

The generator is a three-state machine driven by a linear feedback shift register. ST_IDLE uses the LFSR's pseudo-random sequence to decide the next packet, ST_SEND builds it to an Internet Mix distribution, and ST_WAIT spaces packets apart using intervals drawn from a Pareto distribution ROM. The traffic that comes out therefore carries the statistical shape of a real network rather than a uniform stream.

On the analysis side a header parser lifts the five-tuple flow key out of each packet, and an AMS module estimates the second frequency moment with a Count-sketch. Parallel hash functions and compact counters keep it within a fixed memory budget, and a collision-avoidance interface keeps the counter updates correct when they contend.

The integrated system was checked by gate-level simulation. The generator produces packets at stochastic intervals, and the monitor shows them crossing the 512-bit AXI4-Stream and feeding the F2 computation, with the testbench driving 85 per cent load: 108 Gbps against the bus ceiling of 128.

The 512-bit stream absorbs the bursts without dropping packets, which is what makes the pairing work: the generator can be pushed past the 100 Gbps target and the analysis still keeps up on the same clock.
