Main Project

An HBM-Based 100 Gbps Traffic Replay Engine on FPGA

Presented at the UST-CYCU Joint Research Symposium 2026, University of Santo Tomas, Philippines. Advised by Prof. Yu-Kuen Lai, Department of Electrical Engineering, Chung Yuan Christian University.

Xilinx Alveo U50 HBM2 Corundum Verilog Vivado AXI PCIe 100G Ethernet

The problem

Replaying recorded traffic is how high-speed network equipment gets tested. Software cannot do it at 100 Gbps: packets are handled one at a time on the CPU, so throughput collapses for the short frames that make up most real traffic, and scheduling jitter distorts the very packet spacing the replay is supposed to preserve.

This design moves the whole job into hardware. The trace lives in High Bandwidth Memory on the FPGA card and is replayed without the host touching the transmit path at all.

How it works

The trace is split into 512-byte batches and spread across all sixteen HBM channels, with batch N landing in channel N mod 16. Eight AXI read masters pull it back, each statically owning its own pair of channels, so no arbitration is needed and no two masters ever contend.

A three-stage pipeline reassembles the Ethernet frames. A hardware throttle reproduces the recorded timestamps and gates only the final stage, so prefetching never stalls while the engine waits for the right moment to transmit.

Results

227.800 µs

Zero loss
All frames received by the peer NIC

Verification used a second 100 Gbps NIC on a back-to-back link. In timestamp mode, 998 of 1,000 replayed packets matched the source records field by field; the two that did not appear are malformed entries in the source trace itself. In max-rate mode the receiver counted all 32,768 frames with nothing dropped.
